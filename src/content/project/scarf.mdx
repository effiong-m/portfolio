highlights:
  - Integrated water temperature, environmental temperature, and humidity sensing into the wearable prototype using Arduino based sensors.
  - Developed hardware controls for the heating and humidification subsystems (button and buzzer to control and alert on status) and troubleshot electrical issues to improve system reliability.
  - Added a data collection dashboard to monitor environmental conditions and support selection of operating thresholds for temperature and humidity.
  - Refined the humidification subsystem by incorporating a preliminary water-level sensing system and tracking user motion to determine sloshing.
  
results:
  - Produced a functional prototype capable of actively monitoring and adjusting temperature and humidity based on environmental conditions.
  - Collected and displayed real time environmental, system status, and user action data that could be used to refine operating thresholds and guide future testing of the scarf under cold weather conditions.
  - Presented at the Faculty of Applied Science and Engineering (FASE) Undergraduate Student Research Award (USRA) recipients showcase
  
reflection: 
  This project was my first experience independently developing the electrical system for a wearable device, and it sparked my interest in hands-on electronics and system integration. One of the most interesting parts was diving into research for the water level sensing system. I looked into automotive oil detection systems and fluid sloshing, and adapted those ideas to the scarf’s humidification subsystem. This taught me how adjacent ideas could be applied across different fields and it showed me how valuable cross-disciplinary research can be when solving unfamiliar design problems.
