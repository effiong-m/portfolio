highlights:
  - Translated user needs for children with motor impairments into system (hardware and software) requirements, measurable specifications, and proposed verification methods.
  - Led hardware research, component selection, and soldering using an Arduino Leonardo, Bluetooth communication, status LEDs, and supporting electronics.
  - Developed embedded code for hardware control and communication between the electronic and software systems to convert incoming commands into Nintendo Switch controller inputs through USB HID emulation.
  - Conducted design reviews and verified design choices with PRISM Lab researchers to gather feedback and ensure the final design aligned with user needs.
  - Assisted with 3D printing and integration of the final enclosure for the electronic system.
  
results:
  - Validated the MAGIT Box as a low-cost, portable proof of concept device for translating BCI inputs into Nintendo Switch controls.
  - Met latency, jitter, and accuracy requirements for real-time gameplay, demonstrating reliable communication across the software, Bluetooth, Arduino, and USB interfaces.
  - Transferred the final prototype to the PRISM Lab for continued lab use and future integration with the MINDSET BCI platform.
  
reflection: 
  This was one of the most rewarding projects I worked on because I got to experience building something that could genuinely make an impact in the lives of children. It also gave me experience across the full development lifecycle of an assitive device, from requirements gathering to verification and validation. I totally enjoyed the hands on hardware and 3D printing work. The most challenging parts were selecting components that met the device requirements and establishing a reliable Bluetooth communication between the hardware and software. Working through these definitely taught me a lot about making trade-offs, and the importance of patience and persistence in the design process. Patience and persistence do seem to come up a lot, and that's because they are really important in both wet lab and dry lab experiments.
