highlights:
  - Contributed to a social accessibility framework using three ML models Random Forest (encampment growth prediction), XGBoost (shelter pressure forecasting), and K-Means (clustering of potential 'desert zones' across neighbourhoods)
  - Trained and evaluated the Random Forest model for encampment growth prediction, comparing it with XGBoost during model selection and integrating it into a broader framework for identifying menstrual product access gaps across Toronto.
  - Conducted stakeholder interviews with The Period Purse, Period Pin, Homes First Society, and Help A Girl Out Canada to ensure the project reflected real distribution challenges and community needs.
  - Designed the interactive dashboard using Streamlit.
  

results:
  - Developed a working proof of concept that combined public data, machine learning, and geographic visualization to surface areas of potentially greater unmet menstrual product need across Toronto.
  - Won the 2026 AI4Good Lab Accelerator Award, providing external validation of the project's feasibility, innovation, and potential for impact.
